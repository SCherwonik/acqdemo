# Key Performance Objectives (KPOs)

Shared reference for the plan, midpoint, and annual skills. It is the overview and index for three public publications of Warfighting Acquisition University (WarU), Human Capital Initiatives, which are works of the US Government and not under copyright: the *KPO Factsheet* (rev. 25 Aug 2026), the *Civilian Acquisition Workforce KPO Library* (Aug 2026), and the *KPO Implementation Guidance for Supervisors* (Aug 2026). The library and the guidance are carried in full in this folder as faithful conversions (see "Which file to open"); the factsheet is folded into the guidance. This file keeps only what a drafter needs at a glance. Follow your component's own procedures where they differ.

## Which file to open
- `kpo-library.md`: the model objectives, as tables of Type and Model Objective for every functional area (Auditing and Oversight, Business - Cost Estimating, Business - Financial Management, Contracting, Engineering and Technical Management, Life Cycle Logistics, Program Management, Test and Evaluation), the model supervisory objective, the cross-functional innovation and mission outcome menu, and the tailoring notes. Open it to pick the objective a KPO starts from.
- `kpo-guidance.md`: the supervisor implementation guidance, with the factsheet folded in. It holds the terminology, the SMART table and formula with examples, the performance information types, how KPO performance is assessed, the lifecycle table, and the supervisor checklist. Open it to write the measure and timeframe, to choose the performance information, or to check how a KPO is assessed.
- This file: who is covered, the two types, the formula in one line, the pitfalls, what changes at the midpoint and annual, and where local rules come from.

## Who is covered, and the authority
- Covered: civilian members of the warfighting acquisition workforce, starting with the FY27 performance planning and appraisal cycle (1 Oct 2026).
- Authority: FY26 NDAA Section 826(a) (P.L. 119-60) and a 15 Jun 2026 memo from the Under Secretary for Acquisition and Sustainment giving Department-level direction.
- KPOs sit inside the existing performance system. The guidance says they do not replace component authorities, HR procedures, or merit principles, and should not create unintended pass/fail consequences unless the governing system requires it.
- Performance information on KPOs can also inform assignments, promotions, awards, training, and certification, as one source among others.

## The two types
- **Strategic Outcome**: what the employee delivers. Mission results, operational impact, speed of delivery, readiness, supply-chain resilience, adoption of commercial or innovative solutions, workforce currency in digital and AI skills.
- **Workforce Behavioral**: how the employee delivers it. Acquisition judgment, collaboration, communication, data-informed trade-offs, end-user engagement, responsible risk-taking, continuous learning.
- Each covered plan needs at least one of each. The categories are mandatory; the wording, measures, and timeframe are tailored to the person's role, grade, and duties.

## The SMART writing formula
`[KPO category] + [specific task or behavior] + [measurable outcome] + [timeframe]`

The five SMART criteria, the guidance's example expectations, and the performance information types that show a KPO was met are in `kpo-guidance.md` (Step 3 and Step 4 of the development process). The model objective to start from is in `kpo-library.md`.

## Pitfalls the documents warn against
- **Copying a library objective unchanged.** The library is a model. Tailor it to the actual work, level, and mission.
- **Wording that cannot be measured.** No number, standard, or observable result means no way to show it was met.
- **Vague verbs.** "Assist" and "participate" hide the real contribution unless the expected contribution is spelled out.
- **Process-only measures.** The guidance says assessment rests on outcomes achieved and behaviors demonstrated, not solely on process compliance or finishing assigned activities.
- **Set and forget.** The guidance expects expectations discussed early, progress discussions held and documented during the cycle, and examples refined over time. Revisit KPOs at the midpoint and revise them with the supervisor if the work changes.

## What changes at the midpoint and annual
- KPO performance is assessed on outcomes achieved and behaviors demonstrated, using observable and verifiable performance information. Evidence for a KPO is the same kind of evidence as for any factor: what was done, the measured result, and who benefited.
- At the midpoint, report progress against the KPO's midpoint milestone and the performance information gathered so far. Do not claim the year-end target as achieved.
- At the annual, report the outcome against the target and the behaviors shown. Tag ledger entries with `KPO1` or `KPO2` the same way as `JA1` or `MS1`.
- Scope and complexity, and mission impact, are also part of how supervisors weigh KPO performance (guidance section 7).

## Local rules come from the pay pool overlay
How many KPOs a plan carries, which factor they sit under, how they are labeled, and the label style vary by pay pool. Read `kpo_required`, `kpo_count`, `kpo_labels`, `kpo_factor`, and `plan_label_style` from `paypool.md`. Never assume them. If the overlay says `kpo_required: no`, do not add KPOs to a plan or a statement.
