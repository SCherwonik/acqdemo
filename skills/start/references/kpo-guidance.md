**Implementation guardrails.** KPOs do not replace component authorities, HR procedures, merit principles, bargaining obligations, or established decision-making processes. KPOs provide performance information to support those processes.

## Purpose of this guidance

This document gives supervisors a common Department approach for incorporating KPOs into FY 2027 performance planning and appraisal discussions for covered civilian warfighting acquisition workforce personnel. It explains the KPO framework, how to develop role-appropriate expectations, what performance information to consider, and how KPO performance should inform broader workforce lifecycle decisions consistent with applicable law, policy, and component procedures.

## 1. Purpose

Key Performance Objectives (KPOs) are mandatory enterprise-level performance objectives required for covered civilian members of the warfighting acquisition workforce beginning with the FY 2027 performance planning and appraisal cycle. KPOs are intended to align individual performance with Department priorities, Warfighting Acquisition System objectives, and measurable mission outcomes.

KPOs assess both what employees deliver and how they deliver it. They reinforce outcome-focused acquisition, responsible risk-taking, innovation, collaboration, continuous learning, and warfighter-focused mission execution.

KPOs must be incorporated into performance plans and appraisal processes. KPO performance information should also inform workforce lifecycle decisions, as appropriate, including assignments, promotions, awards and recognition, workforce development, certification, training, and continuous learning.

## 2. What Supervisors Must Know

Supervisors are responsible for translating enterprise KPO requirements into performance expectations that are relevant to the employee's role, grade, duties, organizational mission, and applicable performance management system.

- Enterprise KPO categories are mandatory; individual performance expectations, measures, and performance indicators should be tailored to the employee's work and level of responsibility.
- Each covered employee performance plan must include at least one Strategic Outcome KPO and one Workforce Behavioral KPO.
- KPOs should encourage mission impact, acquisition judgment, innovation, responsible risk-taking, collaboration, and continuous learning.
- Supervisors should use objective and verifiable performance information when assessing KPO achievement.
- KPOs should be implemented consistently with applicable performance management policy, component procedures, and HR guidance.

> **Important implementation note:** KPOs are intended to strengthen outcome-focused performance expectations and should not be implemented in a manner that creates unintended pass/fail consequences unless specifically required by the governing performance management system. Supervisors should coordinate with HR, DACMs/DATMs, or component performance management officials when questions arise.

## 3. Common Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Acquisition Judgment | Application of professional expertise, critical thinking, and sound decision-making to balance mission requirements, risk, cost, schedule, and performance considerations. |
| Adaptive Acquisition Approaches | Methods that accelerate capability delivery through iterative development, rapid prototyping, commercial practices, flexible acquisition pathways, and tailored oversight. |
| End User | The warfighter, operational customer, or mission stakeholder who ultimately receives or uses the capability, product, service, or operational effect. |
| Innovation | Adoption of new approaches, authorities, technologies, or business practices that improve acquisition outcomes, mission effectiveness, or workforce performance. |
| Key Performance Objectives | Mandatory enterprise-level performance objectives that assess contribution to mission outcomes and demonstration of acquisition workforce behaviors. |
| Performance Information | Observable and verifiable information used to assess employee performance, including outcomes, work products, stakeholder feedback, documented decisions, and demonstrated behaviors. |
| Responsible Risk-Taking | Willingness to pursue opportunities and make informed decisions despite uncertainty while appropriately assessing, documenting, and managing potential impacts. |
| Strategic Outcome Objectives | Mission-focused results and organizational outcomes employees are expected to support or achieve. |
| Workforce Behavioral Objectives | Professional behaviors, practices, and decision-making attributes expected of acquisition professionals. |

## 4. KPO Structure

KPOs assess two essential dimensions:

1. Strategic Outcomes Achieved - what the employee delivers.
2. Workforce Behaviors Demonstrated - how the employee delivers it.

Every covered employee must have at least one Strategic Outcome KPO and one Workforce Behavioral KPO. Supervisors may use the KPO Library as a starting point and tailor the performance expectations, measures, and indicators to the employee's duties and mission context.

### 4.1 Strategic Outcome Objectives

Strategic Outcome Objectives assess what was accomplished and how the employee's contribution supported warfighter priorities, Department objectives, or Warfighting Acquisition System outcomes.

- Expeditious delivery of capabilities that enhance operational readiness.
- Integration of innovative solutions, commercial practices, or emerging technologies.
- Supply-chain and industrial-base resilience and surge capacity.
- Improved mission effectiveness, decision quality, speed, or acquisition outcomes.
- Workforce currency in digital, artificial intelligence, technical, or functional competencies.

### 4.2 Workforce Behavioral Objectives

Workforce Behavioral Objectives assess how outcomes were achieved, including the employee's judgment, collaboration, communication, problem solving, risk management, and commitment to learning.

- Appropriate application of innovative acquisition authorities, commercial practices, or adaptive pathways.
- Engagement with end users and incorporation of operational feedback.
- Use of data, analysis, iteration, and trade-off considerations to support decisions.
- Sound acquisition judgment, collaboration, and communication in support of mission outcomes.
- Continuous learning and measurable application of new knowledge or skills.

## 5. Supervisor Responsibilities

Supervisors must:

- Integrate KPOs into each covered employee's performance plan.
- Establish clear expectations for outcomes and behaviors.
- Tailor KPO expectations to the employee's role, grade, duties, and mission context.
- Conduct regular performance discussions and document progress during the rating cycle.
- Collect objective and documented performance information.
- Use KPO performance information, as appropriate, to inform development, assignments, awards and recognition, promotions, and other workforce lifecycle decisions consistent with applicable policy and component procedures.

Supervisors should ensure KPOs reinforce warfighter-focused acquisition practices, innovation, responsible risk-taking, acquisition judgment, and continuous learning.

## 6. KPO Development Process

### Step 1 - Align with Enterprise and Organizational Priorities

Before drafting KPO expectations, identify the Department priorities, Warfighting Acquisition System objectives, and organizational mission outcomes the employee supports.

- Warfighter readiness and operational outcomes.
- Responsiveness to emerging threats and mission needs.
- Rapid delivery of capability.
- Supply-chain and industrial-base resilience.
- Innovation, commercial solutions, and adaptive approaches.

Supervisor question: What organizational priorities should this employee support, and what outcomes and behaviors would demonstrate that contribution?

### Step 2 - Draft Clear and Aligned KPO Expectations

Supervisors do not need to rewrite the enterprise KPO categories. Instead, they should develop performance expectations that connect the employee's daily work to those categories.

- Include at least one Strategic Outcome KPO.
- Include at least one Workforce Behavioral KPO.
- Ensure each expectation is objective, measurable, and appropriate for the employee's role and level of responsibility.
- Tailor the performance information and measures to the employee's duties, organization, and performance management system.

Supervisor question: Given this employee's position, grade, and mission role, what is a reasonable, mission-aligned expectation?

### Step 3 - Apply SMART Criteria

KPO expectations should be written using SMART criteria: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-Bound.

| SMART Criterion | Application | How to Write It |
|---|---|---|
| S - Specific | Direct link to a job task, mission outcome, or workforce behavior. | Define exactly what the employee is expected to do or demonstrate. Avoid vague words such as "assist" or "participate" unless the expected contribution is clear. |
| M - Measurable | Observable quality, quantity, timeliness, frequency, or results. | Define the standard of success and what results or performance information will demonstrate it was met. |
| A - Achievable | Appropriate for the employee's grade, authority, proficiency, and mission context. | Ensure the scope of responsibility aligns with the employee's role and level. |
| R - Relevant | Direct alignment with enterprise KPO categories and organizational priorities. | Explain how the expectation supports mission outcomes or desired workforce behaviors. |
| T - Time-Bound | Set within clear deadlines, milestones, or the appraisal cycle. | Specify when the standard must be met, such as by a milestone, quarter, or throughout the rating cycle. |

SMART writing formula: [Relevant KPO category] + [specific task or behavior] + [measurable outcomes/results] + [timeframe].

Examples:

- Develop an acquisition strategy that leverages market research and stakeholder input to support mission objectives by the end of Q3.
- Engage end users quarterly and incorporate operational feedback into acquisition decisions.
- Assess and recommend appropriate acquisition pathways to support rapid capability delivery by Q2.
- Lead iterative planning efforts and provide data-informed recommendations regarding continuation, adjustment, or termination of initiatives by Q4.

Additional KPO examples are available in the KPO Library and may be adapted to reflect organizational priorities, position requirements, and employee responsibilities.

### Step 4 - Identify Behavioral Indicators and Performance Information

Supervisors should evaluate both what the employee accomplished and how the employee achieved those outcomes. Performance information should be objective, verifiable, and tied directly to outcomes and behaviors.

- Application of innovative acquisition approaches.
- Engagement with end users resulting in measurable or documented improvements.
- Data-informed recommendations and decision-making.
- Adaptive problem solving and iterative execution.
- Continuous learning and professional development.
- Actions that improve mission effectiveness or reduce delivery timelines.

| Performance Information Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Quantitative Metrics | Contract award timelines; cost or schedule variance; milestone completion; small business utilization; protest rates; rework frequency. |
| Qualitative Work Products | Acquisition strategies; source selection documentation; cost estimates; statements of work; briefings; decision papers; reports submitted to leadership. |
| Stakeholder Input | Documented feedback from requiring activities, program offices, legal, resource management, senior leaders, or operational users. |
| Process Indicators | Completeness, accuracy, timeliness, sequencing, documentation quality, and adherence to appropriate review gates or internal controls. |
| Competency Demonstrations | Observable application of DAWIA competencies during market research, source selection, EVM reviews, industry engagement, negotiation, or trade-off decisions. |
| Development and Learning Outcomes | Application of training, mentoring or coaching others, participation in communities of practice, knowledge-sharing, or credential/certification progress. |

Supervisor question: What observable behaviors and performance information best demonstrate acquisition judgment, innovation, collaboration, learning, and mission impact?

## 7. KPO Performance Assessment

Performance should be assessed based on outcomes achieved and behaviors demonstrated; not solely on process compliance or completion of assigned activities. Supervisors should evaluate performance using observable performance information and demonstrated results.

When assessing KPO performance, supervisors should consider:

- Outcomes achieved: mission results delivered, contribution to operational readiness, and advancement of enterprise priorities.
- Behaviors demonstrated: innovation, acquisition judgment, responsible risk-taking, collaboration, stakeholder engagement, and continuous learning.
- Scope and complexity: breadth of responsibility, challenge level, independence, and influence.
- Mission impact: contribution to operational readiness, mission effectiveness, or organizational priorities.

## 8. Informing Talent Management and Workforce Lifecycle Decisions

FY26 NDAA Section 826 requires KPOs to be integrated into performance management and used to inform workforce lifecycle decisions. Supervisors should use KPO performance information as one source of information, consistent with applicable law, policy, merit principles, collective bargaining obligations where applicable, HR guidance, and component procedures.

KPO performance information may inform:

- Performance ratings and performance discussions.
- Cross-functional assignments and career-broadening opportunities.
- Promotion, advancement, and succession planning decisions, where appropriate and consistent with applicable procedures.
- Awards, recognition, and other incentives.
- Certification, training, continuous learning, and workforce development opportunities.

| Lifecycle Area | KPO Integration Point | Intended Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Performance Management | Translate enterprise KPO categories into role-appropriate performance expectations and assess outcomes and behaviors during the appraisal cycle. | Improves alignment between individual performance and warfighter-focused mission outcomes. |
| Assignments and Career Broadening | Consider demonstrated KPO performance when identifying employees for assignments, rotations, or broader responsibility. | Helps match demonstrated strengths and development needs to mission requirements. |
| Promotions and Advancement | Use KPO performance, where appropriate and consistent with applicable procedures, as one factor informing promotion, advancement, and succession planning decisions. | Supports performance-informed decisions about readiness for greater responsibility. |
| Awards and Recognition | Consider mission impact, innovation, responsible risk-taking, collaboration, and measurable outcomes when evaluating awards and recognition. | Reinforces desired behaviors and outcomes without creating a one-size-fits-all awards requirement. |
| Training and Continuous Learning | Use KPO performance discussions to identify certification, training, continuous learning, and development needs. | Supports a technically proficient, agile, and mission-focused workforce. |
| Continuous Review | Periodically review implementation experience and refine examples, performance information guidance, and support materials as needed. | Maintains alignment with Department priorities and improves implementation over time. |

## 9. Coordination with HR, DACMs, and DATMs

Supervisors should coordinate with HR advisors, Directors of Acquisition Career Management, Directors of Acquisition Talent Management, and component performance management officials when applying KPO information to performance management or broader workforce lifecycle decisions. OUSW(P&R), in coordination with OUSW(A&S), will provide separate communication to the HR community addressing how KPOs should be reflected in performance management and considered, as appropriate, in assignments, promotions, awards and recognition, training, certification, and continuous learning.

## 10. Supervisor Checklist

- Identify the enterprise and organizational priorities the employee supports.
- Select or tailor at least one Strategic Outcome KPO and one Workforce Behavioral KPO.
- Write expectations using SMART criteria and align them to the employee's duties and level of responsibility.
- Discuss expectations with the employee early in the performance cycle.
- Identify performance information that will be used to assess outcomes and behaviors.
- Conduct progress discussions and document performance results and relevant supporting information during the rating cycle.
- Use KPO performance information to inform workforce development and lifecycle decisions consistent with applicable policy and procedures.
