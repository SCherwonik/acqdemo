# Brain Dump Worksheet

Fill this in offline, in Word or Notepad, over several days. Then upload it, or paste it in, and the
assistant will turn it into ledger entries and ask follow-up questions about the thin parts.

This worksheet does the job that a long spoken answer does on a voice tool. Typed answers are
shorter than spoken ones, so nothing here asks you to start from a blank page. Almost every prompt
is a list to tick, a month to fill, or a name to add. Ticking one box and writing four words next to
it is worth more than a paragraph you never write.

## How to use it

1. Save your own copy. Type directly under each prompt, on the blank lines.
2. Do it in several sittings. Part 3, the tick lists, is the fastest start when you are cold.
3. Keep your calendar, sent mail, and shared drive open beside you. Recognition beats recall.
4. Skip anything that does not apply. Write "skip" or leave it blank. Nothing here is required.
5. When you are done, or when you have had enough, upload the file.

## Rules for what you write here

- **Estimated numbers are fine.** Write the estimate and write how you got there. "About 40 hours
  saved a month, basis: 8 analysts times 5 hours each" is usable. "About 40 hours" on its own is not.
  If you are not sure, put the low end you can defend and say so.
- **Names are welcome here.** This worksheet is private working material. Names go in your notes and
  help the assistant count people, organizations, and audiences. Names never appear in the final
  assessment text that goes into CAS2Net.
- **Never write anything classified.** Not here, not in a calendar export, not in the conversation.
  If a project name or a meeting title is sensitive, call it "Program A" and say what it was instead.
- **Do not invent anything.** No events, no audiences, no awards that did not happen. A short honest
  worksheet beats a padded one, because everything you write gets traced back to you.
- **Rambling is fine.** Write in fragments. Jump around. The assistant will sort it out.

## What a filled entry looks like

This is fictional, and it is the depth to aim for on your two or three biggest items. Most entries
will be shorter than this, and that is fine.

> **Workstream name:** Contract closeout backlog
>
> **What I did:** Took over the section's closeout backlog in January and rebuilt the tracker so
> each file shows its own next action and due date instead of one list of everything.
>
> **My role:** owned
>
> **Who used it or received it:** the 5 specialists in my section, plus the two division offices
> that send us files. Briefed it once to the branch chief, Pat Example.
>
> **Before and after:** Before, closeouts sat about 11 months on average and nobody could say which
> file was next. After, average is about 4 months and the oldest file is visible every Monday.
>
> **Numbers, and how I got them:** 212 files closed in the cycle, basis: count from the tracker on
> 12 Sep. About 7 months faster per file, basis: tracker average before (11 months) minus after
> (4 months). About $3.1M deobligated and returned, basis: the finance summary from June.
>
> **What almost stopped it:** two of the five specialists moved out in the spring, so I covered the
> aerospace files myself for about three months.
>
> **What decision or mission it fed:** the deobligated funds went back to the squadron for
> unfunded requirements in the June review.
>
> **Will it keep happening:** yes, the Monday tracker review is now standing in the section.
>
> **Where proof lives:** the tracker file on the shared drive, the June finance summary email,
> my Monday calendar series.

# Part 6. Go and look

These take ten minutes each and consistently recover work that memory drops. Do as many as you can
stand. Write what you find; titles alone are enough.

- **Sent mail.** Search your sent items for "attached", "brief", "slides", "thanks", and "final".
  Read the subject lines for the rating period and write down the ones that were real work:

- **What did you brief?** Open your calendar and list every meeting where you presented, demoed, or
  led rather than attended:

- **What did you publish or write?** Slide decks, reports, memos, procedures, guides, policies,
  spreadsheets others use. Titles only:

- **What did you hand off?** Anything another person or office now runs because you built it,
  documented it, or trained them on it:

- **What did you fix?** Anything that was broken, wrong, or failing that works now:

- **What did you automate?** Anything that used to be done by hand:

- **Shared drives and repositories.** Which folders or repositories did you touch most this cycle,
  and what is in them:

- **Chat and channels.** Which threads or channels were busiest for you, and what were they about:

- **Awards, thanks, and requests.** Any email thanking you, citing your work, or asking for more of
  it. Who sent it and what for:

# Part 7. Anything else

The most useful answers usually arrive after the lists are done. Write whatever comes to mind now,
in any order, in any state.

- What are you proudest of this cycle?

- What took the most time?

- What would have failed without you?

- What do you think your supervisor does not know about?

- What did you do that does not fit anywhere above?

# When you are finished

Save the file, then upload it or paste it in. You do not have to finish it first. A half filled
worksheet is a good starting point, and the assistant will ask about the gaps rather than leave
them. Anything you remember later can be added at any time and folded in.
