# Key Performance Objectives (KPOs)

Shared reference for the contribution plan, the midpoint, and the annual. It summarizes three public publications of Warfighting Acquisition University (WarU), Human Capital Initiatives: the *KPO Factsheet* (rev. 25 Aug 2026), the *Civilian Acquisition Workforce KPO Library* (Aug 2026), and the *KPO Implementation Guidance for Supervisors* (Aug 2026). It is a summary, not a copy. Read the originals for exact wording, and follow your component's own procedures where they differ.

## Who is covered, and the authority
- Covered: civilian members of the warfighting acquisition workforce, starting with the FY27 performance planning and appraisal cycle (1 Oct 2026).
- Authority: FY26 NDAA Section 826(a) (P.L. 119-60) and a 15 Jun 2026 memo from the Under Secretary for Acquisition and Sustainment giving Department-level direction.
- KPOs sit inside the existing performance system. The guidance says they do not replace component authorities, HR procedures, or merit principles, and should not create unintended pass/fail consequences unless the governing system requires it.
- Performance information on KPOs can also inform assignments, promotions, awards, training, and certification, as one source among others.

## The two types
- **Strategic Outcome**: what the employee delivers. Mission results, operational impact, speed of delivery, readiness, supply-chain resilience, adoption of commercial or innovative solutions, workforce currency in digital and AI skills.
- **Workforce Behavioral**: how the employee delivers it. Acquisition judgment, collaboration, communication, data-informed trade-offs, end-user engagement, responsible risk-taking, continuous learning.
- Each covered plan needs at least one of each. The categories are mandatory; the wording, measures, and timeframe are tailored to the person's role, grade, and duties.

## The SMART writing formula
`[KPO category] + [specific task or behavior] + [measurable outcome] + [timeframe]`

- Specific: tie it to a real task, outcome, or behavior.
- Measurable: state quality, quantity, timeliness, frequency, or result, and what information will show it was met.
- Achievable: within the employee's grade, authority, and proficiency.
- Relevant: connects to the KPO category and the organization's priorities.
- Time-bound: a milestone, a quarter, or the appraisal cycle.

## Performance information that shows a KPO was met
It should be observable, verifiable, and tied to both outcomes and behaviors.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Quantitative metrics | award timelines, cost or schedule variance, milestones met, rework frequency, protest rates |
| Work products | strategies, estimates, decision papers, briefings, reports to leadership |
| Stakeholder input | documented feedback from program offices, requiring activities, legal, resource managers, users |
| Process indicators | completeness, accuracy, timeliness, documentation quality, review gates |
| Competency demonstrations | observable use of DAWIA competencies in market research, source selection, negotiation, trade-offs |
| Learning outcomes | training applied, mentoring others, communities of practice, certification progress |

| Auditing and Oversight | SO: timely oversight that protects resources while enabling execution; risk-based oversight balancing mission need, compliance, and efficiency. WB: raise high-risk issues early with actionable recommendations; resolve findings with acquisition teams while keeping independence. |
| Business, Cost Estimating | SO: timely, accurate estimates that support fast decisions; spot major cost drivers early and recommend options. WB: data-driven recommendations on scope or trade-offs, including stopping misaligned efforts; benchmark against commercial, historical, or analogous data. |
| Business, Financial Management | SO: align funding execution to capability delivery with fiscal discipline; timely financial analysis for trade-offs and prioritization. WB: keep clear visibility of cost, schedule, and funding; find financial approaches that remove barriers to innovative or adaptive acquisition. |

WB: propose contracting approaches that improve speed, competition, or affordability; collaborate with program, user, legal, and resource stakeholders; use data to catch cost, schedule, quality, or vendor risks early. |
| Engineering and Technical Management | SO: speed technical maturation, integration, or verification; integrate innovative, commercial, digital, or emerging technology. WB: use data, modeling, and trade-off analysis in technical decisions, including adjusting or stopping work; bring end-user feedback into priorities and designs. |

| Cross-functional menu | SO: consider commercial solutions before bespoke development; use innovative authorities and adaptive pathways; focus on end-user outcomes. WB: iterate and make data-driven trade-offs; complete role-relevant digital, data, or AI learning and apply it; take and document responsible risk. |

## Pitfalls the documents warn against
- **Copying a library objective unchanged.** The library is a model. Tailor it to the actual work, level, and mission.
- **Wording that cannot be measured.** No number, standard, or observable result means no way to show it was met.
- **Vague verbs.** "Assist" and "participate" hide the real contribution unless the expected contribution is spelled out.
- **Process-only measures.** The guidance says assessment rests on outcomes achieved and behaviors demonstrated, not solely on process compliance or finishing assigned activities.
- **Set and forget.** The guidance expects expectations discussed early, progress discussions held and documented during the cycle, and examples refined over time. Revisit KPOs at the midpoint and revise them with the supervisor if the work changes.

## What changes at the midpoint and annual
- KPO performance is assessed on outcomes achieved and behaviors demonstrated, using observable and verifiable performance information. Evidence for a KPO is the same kind of evidence as for any factor: what was done, the measured result, and who benefited.
- At the midpoint, report progress against the KPO's midpoint milestone and the performance information gathered so far. Do not claim the year-end target as achieved.
- At the annual, report the outcome against the target and the behaviors shown. Tag ledger entries with `KPO1` or `KPO2` the same way as `JA1` or `MS1`.
- Scope and complexity, and mission impact, are also part of how supervisors weigh KPO performance.

## Local rules come from the pay pool overlay
How many KPOs a plan carries, which factor they sit under, how they are labeled, and the label style vary by pay pool. Read `kpo_required`, `kpo_count`, `kpo_labels`, `kpo_factor`, and `plan_label_style` from the `# Pay pool` section of the workspace block. Never assume them, and if a line is missing, ask the user. If the overlay says `kpo_required: no`, do not add KPOs to a plan or a statement.
